# The design is a working agreement

Stand where the maker will stand. Can a capable person make the intended thing from this handoff without inventing its purpose, ignoring a difficult objection or guessing which choice the user approved? Preserve the decisions that make the concept itself, the constraints that protect the user and the uncertainty that could change the route.

A useful handoff carries the original request, the agreed outcome and audience, the chosen concept and rationale, actual behavior or experience, relationships and interfaces, constraints, alternatives and their dispositions, source/evidence access, assumptions and conditional branches, and observations that would distinguish success from a fluent near miss. Adapt the depth to the domain. A physical object may need dimensions and materials; a story may need voice, dramatic structure and canon; neither benefits from invented software endpoints.

Name the actual design revision by a commit, bounded snapshot or file identity appropriate to its mutability. Do not create an elaborate custody system for a short brief. Preserve material dissent and the evidence that could change the choice. A proposal is still a proposal; an observation is not user approval; design completion does not grant implementation authority.

Enter BRAID when the user's request includes making and the remaining choices fit that grant. Transfer testable uncertainties as experiments and explain what their outcomes would change. When a consequential reserved choice is unresolved, continue independent reversible portions and surface the specific decision. A generic “approve the design” gate is unnecessary when the owner already authorized design and construction and the result remains inside that scope.

Re-entry from BRAID is a delta: what was discovered, which design assumption it affects, what options remain, what work can proceed and who can decide. The return does not erase the rest of the design. Preserve the new revision and pass its consequences to CHECKER as well as MAKER.
