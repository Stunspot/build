# Choose and guide your workflow

[BUILD home](https://stunspot.github.io/build/) · [Install and start](start.md) · [Recovery](recovery.md)

BUILD 0.1.0 includes complete CONCEIVE and BRAID snapshots. You can request either workflow directly through BUILD. Use the smallest workflow that serves the outcome.

## Design and make: BUILD

Use BUILD for an idea that needs to become a finished result. It can move a settled design straight to BRAID or use existing evidence without repeating unnecessary design work.

```text
Create a BUILD called Workshop Kit: design and make a printable facilitator guide for a 45-minute neighborhood repair workshop. Assume twelve adults, basic hand tools and no internet at the venue. Include a clear stopping point and a checklist for the facilitator. Do not publish it or buy anything.
```

This full named launch requests five communicating design chats followed by three execution chats and one recurring SUPERVISOR check, normally every ten minutes until completion. Your scope and host constraints govern. Add `Do not schedule unattended supervision` if that is your limit.

## Design only: CONCEIVE

Use CONCEIVE when the important work is deciding what the thing should be. Its five contexts consider possibility, integration, practical circumstances, evidence and longer-term consequences. Their centers of attention overlap; they can revise one another's ideas.

```text
Create a CONCEIVE named Workshop Kit to design a 45-minute neighborhood repair workshop in five communicating chats. Assume twelve adults, basic hand tools and no internet at the venue. Deliver the design, its reasons, unresolved conditions and next action. Stop at design; do not implement or publish.
```

Expect five real contexts if the host can supply them, initial independent readings, an exchange that changes the candidate design, and a synthesis each participant can challenge. A useful conditional design or a decision not to build can be the result. Design-only instructions do not authorize making.

## Make and check: BRAID

Use BRAID when you already know the objective and have the design or brief ready. Provide the actual material, not just a path inaccessible to the collaborators.

```text
Create a BRAID to do Workshop Kit: turn this approved workshop design into a printable facilitator guide and participant checklist. Preserve the stated time, audience and tool constraints. Check whether a facilitator can follow the sequence and tell when the workshop is complete. Do not publish it or use paid services. Do not schedule unattended supervision.
```

MAKER owns the deliverable, CHECKER independently judges it, and SUPERVISOR directs the effort. The invocation chat launches those three roles; it is not a fourth strand. A standard BRAID launch includes recurring supervision unless you narrow it, as this example does.

## Set useful constraints

Name the result, intended user, operating conditions and observable success. Include existing work and non-negotiable decisions. State any budget, deadline, delivery location or reserved decision that can change the work. You do not need to pre-design every implementation detail.

For example: `Keep this local. Use existing tools. Stop before any paid action. Ask me before publishing. If the scope cannot fit my budget, preserve completed work and explain the smallest useful result.` A budget instruction is a constraint for the workflow, not a guaranteed billing cap enforced by BUILD.

## Understand cost and context

Five design contexts and three execution roles can consume more time and model usage than one chat. Recurring supervision consumes additional runs. Actual cost depends on the host, model, plan, amount of context, tools and duration. BUILD does not provide a model or included quota and does not promise savings.

Use CONCEIVE alone for a design decision, BRAID alone for settled execution, or an explanation before launching. Give collaborators the context they need while excluding unrelated private material and secrets. Use the host's usage controls for enforceable account limits.

## Judge progress and completion

**PREPARED** means briefs are ready. **CREATED** means real collaborator identities exist but coordination is not yet established. **ACTIVE** requires observed access and exchanges. These describe collaboration; they are not an independent verdict on the result.

The handoff into making should preserve the original objective, selected design revision, settled choices, constraints, uncertainty and acceptance observations. If a material assumption fails during making, BUILD returns the precise question and evidence to design while preserving unaffected work.

Completion means the requested result exists at the authorized delivery surface, the delivered revision matches the applicable CHECKER verdict, and material remaining risks are explicit. An agreed design, a passing package check or three busy chats does not establish that result.
